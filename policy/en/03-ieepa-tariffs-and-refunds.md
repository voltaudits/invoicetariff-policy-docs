# IEEPA Tariffs Struck Down & How to Get Refunds (CAPE)

> As of **2026-10-05**. Tariff policy changes frequently — see the [Tariff Radar (change log + email alerts)](https://invoicetariff.com/en/radar) for the latest.
> 其他语言：[中文](../zh/03-ieepa-tariffs-and-refunds.md)

## What the IEEPA tariffs were

In 2025 the U.S. government imposed two rounds of tariffs under the **International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA)**:

- **"Fentanyl" tariffs** — imposed on specific countries citing the fentanyl issue;
- **"Reciprocal" tariffs** — country-by-country tiers imposed on trading partners.

Both were reported at entry through Chapter 99 headings **9903.01 / 9903.02**, stacking on top of MFN, Section 301 and the other layers.

## 2026-02-20: the Supreme Court strikes them down

**On February 20, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court held all IEEPA tariffs unlawful in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump***. CBP assessed the IEEPA layer through **2026-02-24**; since then:

- New entries **no longer** carry the 9903.01 / 9903.02 layer — that stack is gone from new entries;
- **Everything outside IEEPA is unaffected**: the China Section 301 lists (Lists 1–4A), the [global Section 301 program](05-global-section-301.md) effective 2026-07-24, Section 232, and AD/CVD are all still collected.

Common misreading: "the tariffs were struck down = nothing is owed anymore." Only the IEEPA layer fell. Many goods still carry MFN + 301 + 232 — run the numbers before celebrating.

## Who can claim a refund

Duties paid under the IEEPA headings (9903.01 / 9903.02) **between 2025-02-04 and 2026-02-24** are refundable through the **CBP CAPE process**. The scale is unprecedented — CBP estimates roughly **$166 billion** in duties across **more than 53 million** entry summaries (91 FR 63299), and built an automated refund tool to handle it (see below).

Key points:

- **Only the IEEPA layer is refunded.** The MFN, 301, 232, AD/CVD amounts on the same entry, plus MPF/HMF fees, are **not** refundable.
- Whether headings 9903.01 / 9903.02 were reported at entry is the direct evidence that IEEPA duty was paid — pull your entry summaries (CBP Form 7501) and check line by line.
- The **importer of record** on the formal entry is the claimant; if a broker files for you, the broker initiates and tracks the claim.

## What CAPE is and how it works

**CAPE (Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries)** is the automated refund tool CBP built for this refund wave; you reach it through the **ACE (Automated Commercial Environment) portal**:

- **CAPE Declaration filing**: submit a CSV listing your entry summary numbers — **up to 9,999 entries per declaration**; multiple entry summaries for the same importer consolidate into one submission and one refund, and you can file as many declarations as needed.
- **Who may file**: only ACE account holders. If a broker handles it, make sure the broker is designated as the notify party in your ACE account.
- **Refunds via ACH direct deposit**: money goes straight to the importer of record's account — CBP **no longer issues paper checks** as a rule (electronic refunds interim final rule, 91 FR 21). Refunds are held if no ACH account is on file in ACE; a check requires a written waiver request to frn-achrefundsupport@cbp.dhs.gov.
- **Interest and entry status**: refunds accrue interest and are issued as quickly as the court orders allow. Unliquidated entries are liquidated/reliquidated without IEEPA duties under the court's orders; liquidated entries must go through the reliquidation window — which path and deadline apply depends on your 7501's status, and the site's estimator flags it per entry.

## Steps

1. **Scope the window**: review only entries filed between 2025-02-04 and 2026-02-24.
2. **Find the IEEPA lines**: on each 7501, locate headings 9903.01 / 9903.02 and the duty deposited against them.
3. **File a CAPE Declaration through ACE**: assemble the entry summary list (CSV, ≤9,999 per declaration), your ACH account details and the notify-party designation; if a broker handles it, have them file and track each submission.
4. **Keep records and watch timing**: refunds accrue interest and finalize at liquidation — log claim dates and amounts per entry and follow through to payment.

To estimate what you can recover, and check eligibility and timing, use the site's free **[IEEPA refund estimator](https://invoicetariff.com/en/ieepa-refund)** (runs entirely in your browser).

## Will things change again

The refund machinery is still evolving: on 2026-10-05 CBP submitted the "Court-Ordered Refunds under the IEEPA Worksheet" collection for PRA extension review to OMB (91 FR 63299, comment window through 2026-11-04), and refund pacing and case-by-case guidance may still move — the [Tariff Radar](https://invoicetariff.com/en/radar) scans official sources every 12 hours; [subscribe to email alerts](https://invoicetariff.com/en/radar) for first-hand changes with Federal Register citations.
